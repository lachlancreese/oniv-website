1 · Why a bottle with your label

A gift that gets opened, poured and talked about.

Most corporate gifts are forgotten by January. A bottle of good Australian wine with your business on the label ends up on someone's table over the holidays, in front of their family and friends, with your name on it.

  • It's personal. Your logo, a Christmas message and the year on every bottle, designed with you.
  • It's practical. One gift works for clients, suppliers, staff and your own end-of-year party.
  • It's kept. A dated, labelled bottle is the kind of gift people photograph, share and hold on to.

4 · Choosing the wine

Something for every table.

Christmas in Australia is summer, so bubbles, whites and rosé get opened on the day, while a good red is the gift people put away for later. If you can't decide, a case of sparkling and a case of red covers most tables.

Sparkling and rosé

Brut NV: fine bubbles, crisp apple and citrus, and a touch of toast. For toasts and arrivals.

Rosé: dry, pale and fresh, with strawberry and watermelon. For summer events and long lunches.

White

Chardonnay, Sauvignon Blanc, Pinot Grigio and Riesling. Chilled, with seafood and salads: the Christmas lunch whites.

Red

Shiraz, Cabernet Sauvignon, Grenache and Pinot Noir. Shiraz is full and generous with dark plum and pepper; Pinot Noir is light and silky, for a summer meal.

Mixing it up

Each case is one wine, so mix by the case: one of sparkling and two of red, for example. Your labels can match across every wine.

Not sure which?

We send the current range, with tasting notes, alongside your mockup, and help you choose on the call.

Think about who's drinking it and when. Bubbles for a toast at the party, whites and rosé for Christmas lunch, and a red for the client who'll put it away for a special dinner.

7 · Giving it well

A few things worth knowing.

  • Gifts of alcohol are for adults. Wine can only be supplied to, and received by, people aged 18 or over.
  • Check gift policies. Government agencies and some businesses limit or record the gifts their people can accept. A quick check avoids an awkward return.
  • Add a personal touch. A handwritten card with each bottle for your key clients goes a long way.
  • Hand it over in person where you can. It's a reason to catch up before the year ends.
  • Store it well. Keep the cases somewhere cool until you give them, away from the heat of a car or a sunny office.

8 · Your checklist

Ten steps to Christmas sorted.

  1. Write your list in groups: key clients, clients, suppliers, staff and the party.
  2. Decide how many bottles each group gets.
  3. Add them up and round up to whole cases of 12.
  4. Set your budget: from $288 a case, with delivery included from two cases.
  5. Choose your wines by the case: sparkling, white, rosé or red.
  6. Find your best logo file, or decide on the words for a type-only label.
  7. Write your Christmas line and your back-label message.
  8. Send your enquiry for a free mockup at oniv.com.au.
  9. Check the proof carefully, then approve it by 14 December (3 December for WA and NT).
  10. Plan who hands over each bottle, and the cards to go with them.
